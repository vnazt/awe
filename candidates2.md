# V2 candidates — second-person rebuild (22 Nov 2026)

Design brief (from research-top3-v2.md): second person from line 1; open
already at rest / on the way home; one staccato sound-fragment line as a
metronome; the dwarfing turn in the last third at geological/cosmic scale,
closing with a short line back to "you"; two holding layers (a trusted
human + an impersonal force); ultra-concrete nouns; 262-280 chars.

## Candidate A — The night train (275 chars) — CHOSEN

```
You are asleep on the night train.
Your mother tucks her coat under your head.
Click. A long pause. Click. The rails.
The carriage rocks, slow.
Below, a river has cut its gorge
through rock older than any road,
and is cutting still, under you,
and the night carries you home.
```

Why it wins: the judge rides as "you" from the first line; the click of
the rails is a pure metronome with no subordinate clauses; the gorge is
geological scale and still being cut now, so the dwarfing image keeps
growing; mother + night are two distinct holding layers; a train is a
scene none of the top texts use.

## Candidate B — The ferry deck (276 chars)

```
You are asleep on the ferry deck.
Your grandfather wraps you in his oilskin.
Slap. A slow slap. Water on the hull.
The deck lifts. The deck settles.
The moon has drawn this tide
since long before there were harbours,
and draws it still, under you,
and it carries you to shore.
```

Strong tide/moon turn, but grandfather + boat sits close to the winning
cluster's territory, so it risks reading as an echo rather than a voice.

## Candidate C — The porch in rain (275 chars)

```
You are asleep on the porch in the rain.
Your mother lays a blanket over your knees.
Drip. A long drip. Rain on the tin roof.
The gutter runs. The roof ticks.
This rain fell once as snow
on peaks that have no names,
and will fall again, long after,
and it rocks you to sleep.
```

Fresh scene and a good drip metronome, but the dwarfing turn (rain that
was once snow) is the most indirect of the three — the scale has to be
inferred rather than felt.

## V3 decision (22 Nov 2026, ~01:25 WIB) — CHOSEN: B, the ferry deck

V2 "The night train" scored 83.3509% (rank 208) — below v1's 84.6635% —
so the freshness-first pick lost. The evidence says this judge rewards
closeness to the proven winner pattern over novelty. B is the closest
to the pattern: the moon-tide is a cosmic force that physically moves
under you (like #1's rising hills, #5's turning earth), while v2's
gorge was scenery below you, not a holder. One revision for v3: the
close changed from "and it carries you to shore." to "and it holds
you till dawn." — the winners close in rest, not arrival, and v2's
"carries you home" close shared the same arrival shape.

## Candidate D — The hammock (fresh draft for the v3 comparison, not chosen)

```
You are asleep in the hammock.
Your father ties the ropes, low.
Sway. A long sway. Rope on the bark.
The shade moves. The shade settles.
These oaks have stood here
since before there was a road,
and stand still, over you,
and the oaks hold you till dusk.
```

Fresh scene (oaks as the holder is unused by the winners) and a clean
sway metronome, but the dwarfing scale is local — oaks predating a road
— against B's moon pulling the whole sea, and "the shade moves" is a
softer holder than a tide lifting the deck under you.
